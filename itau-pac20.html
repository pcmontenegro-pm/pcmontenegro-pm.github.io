PAC 2.0 — Automated Collections

Redesigning a recurring collections platform to improve payment recovery, failure visibility and operational control at Chile's largest private bank.

Senior Product Owner Recurring Payments Collections Infrastructure

Executive Summary

Collections that failed silently.

PAC 2.0 was a platform-level redesign of Itaú Chile's recurring collections system. The existing platform had no structured retry logic, no failure classification, and limited visibility into what was happening when a collection attempt failed. Problems surfaced through customer complaints and operational escalation — not through monitoring.

The initiative introduced a failure taxonomy, redesigned the retry logic, and built operational monitoring that gave product, operations and commercial teams a shared view of collection performance for the first time.

"The real cost of silent failures is that nobody knows what to fix."

The Problem

Complaints were the only monitoring system.

When a collection attempt failed, nothing happened automatically. There was no classification to identify why it failed, no logic to decide whether to retry, and no alert to notify the operational team. The failure only surfaced when a customer complained or an operational team member noticed something was wrong.

This reactive model had a direct impact on collection rates, operational workload, and customer satisfaction. Teams were spending significant time investigating cases that could have been resolved automatically — or at least identified much earlier.

Product Challenge

What made it difficult.

01 Legacy system constraints: The existing collections infrastructure had limited configurability. New logic had to be layered carefully without disrupting active payment cycles.
02 Failure code complexity: Defining a failure taxonomy required understanding the bank's specific error codes, their causes, and which were retriable — which varied by payment type and bank relationship.
03 Multiple stakeholder interests: Operations wanted fewer manual cases. Commercial wanted higher collection rates. Risk wanted conservative retry logic. Aligning these into a single operating model required deliberate trade-off decisions.
04 Measuring success at baseline: There was no existing baseline for key metrics. Establishing the measurement framework was a product deliverable in itself, before any improvement could be tracked.
My Role

Defining the operating model.

I led the product definition across the full scope of PAC 2.0: failure taxonomy, retry logic design, dashboard requirements, and the operating model for how product, operations and commercial teams would interact with the new system.

This included running the discovery work to understand the existing failure modes, working with engineering to define what was technically feasible within the existing infrastructure, and coordinating risk and compliance sign-off on the retry configuration.

Key Decisions

What I decided.

01 Taxonomy before retry logic: Defined the failure classification framework before designing retry rules — ensuring retries were only applied to cases where they could realistically succeed.
02 Conservative initial retry parameters: Recommended starting with conservative timing and frequency parameters to limit risk during the first release, with a clear plan to tune based on observed outcomes.
03 Dashboard designed for three audiences: The operational view, the commercial view and the product view required different data cuts. Rather than one complex dashboard, I defined three purpose-specific views.
04 Baseline measurement before launch: Established the metrics framework before go-live — ensuring we had a true before/after comparison rather than directional estimates.

European Relevance

Why this translates to European roles.

SEPA Direct Debit retry management
PAC 2.0's failure taxonomy and retry logic is directly equivalent to R-transaction management under SEPA — the same discipline of classifying rejection reasons (AM04, MD01, AC01) and defining recovery strategies per failure type.
Collections intelligence & monitoring
Any recurring payment product — subscription billing, insurance, utilities — faces the same challenge: how do you know when collections are failing, why they're failing, and what to do about it? PAC 2.0 answers all three.
Risk-aware product delivery
Defining retry parameters in a regulated environment — balancing commercial recovery goals with compliance requirements — demonstrates the kind of PM judgement that European banking and fintech roles require.
Product Learnings

What this shows about how I work.

Visibility is a product feature. The dashboard wasn't an afterthought — it was a core deliverable. Operational teams can only manage what they can see, and product teams can only improve what they can measure.

Classification before automation. Applying retry logic to all failures regardless of type would have created noise, wasted resources, and potentially frustrated customers. Getting the taxonomy right first made the automation meaningful.

Shared data creates aligned teams. The most durable outcome of PAC 2.0 wasn't the retry logic — it was the shared operational view. When product, operations and commercial teams work from the same data, escalations decrease and improvement becomes collaborative.

Conservative first releases are smart releases. Starting with conservative retry parameters — rather than optimising for maximum recovery from day one — limited risk and built the internal credibility needed to iterate confidently afterward.
