Executive Summary

What Monevol 360 is.

Monevol 360 is a personal finance mobile app concept built for the underbanked and financially underserved population in Latin America — a segment that represents hundreds of millions of users with smartphones but without meaningful access to tools that help them manage money effectively.

The product was conceived, researched, designed and validated end-to-end — from 34 qualitative user interviews across Chile, Colombia and Argentina through to a fully tested interactive prototype. This case study documents that process as a product strategy exercise, not a design portfolio piece.

"Most people with irregular income don't fail because they spend too much. They fail because they can't see what's happening until it's too late."

Market Problem

Financial exclusion isn't a banking problem.
It's a product problem.

Across Latin America, a large and growing share of the population has access to a smartphone — but not to tools that meaningfully help them manage their financial lives. Existing fintech products are designed for salaried, banked users. They assume stable income, credit history, and financial literacy that most of this segment simply doesn't have.

The result: people who need financial tools the most are served by the worst ones — spreadsheets, WhatsApp notes, and pure memory. The market gap is not access to banking. It's access to understanding their own money.

User Insight · 34 Interviews · Chile, Colombia, Argentina

Five behavioural pain points
that define the opportunity.

Research was conducted across 34 interviews in three countries, validated with card sorting (11 participants) and competitive benchmarking against 6 comparable products. The findings converged on five consistent behavioural patterns — each representing a specific product opportunity.

Invisible spending patterns
Users couldn't explain where their money went. Not because of recklessness — but because no tool showed them. Expense visibility was the single most-requested capability.
"I know I spend a lot but I couldn't tell you on what. It just disappears."
Unstable, irregular income
Many participants combined part-time work, freelance income, and informal gigs. Monthly budgeting tools built for fixed salaries didn't fit. The product needed to handle income volatility natively.
"I make different money each month. Apps for saving assume I know what I'll earn."
No savings discipline or system
Saving was viewed as what's left over — not a planned behaviour. Without a dedicated system, savings were the first casualty of unexpected expenses. Goal-based framing was the key motivational trigger.
"I always say I'll save. Then something comes up and it's gone."
Low financial literacy, high desire to learn
Users expressed strong interest in financial education — but existing content was either too complex or too generic. Contextual, bite-sized tips triggered by their own financial behaviour were highly valued.
"I want to understand money better. But finance apps talk like bankers."
Reactive not proactive financial decisions
Without alerts or early warnings, users only noticed budget problems after they happened. A proactive alert system — one that surfaces risk before the crisis — was consistently rated as the most useful potential feature.
"By the time I realise I've overspent, it's already too late to fix it."

Product Strategy

Why this segment. Why this solution.
Why now.

Why this segment: Low-to-middle income users in LATAM are the fastest-growing smartphone-connected population — and the most underserved by existing fintech. They don't lack the need or the intent to manage money better. They lack a tool built for their actual context: irregular income, cash-adjacent behaviour, low financial vocabulary, and zero tolerance for complexity.

Why this solution: Behaviour change, not feature overload. Research consistently showed that users wanted clarity, not complexity. A dashboard that tells them the truth about their money — simply, without judgment — outperforms any sophisticated financial planning tool they'll never actually use.

Why now: Mobile penetration in LATAM has crossed the inflection point. WhatsApp-native behaviours have normalised daily financial conversations on mobile. Neobanks like Nubank have proven willingness-to-pay among underserved users. The rails are ready. The behaviour is ready. The product needed to be built.

MVP Prioritisation · Impact vs Complexity

What goes in V1.
And what waits.

Prioritisation was driven by one constraint: the first version must change behaviour without requiring behavioural change to use it. Features that required high cognitive load or external data (bank connections, tax systems) were deferred. Features that delivered immediate value with minimal friction were prioritised.

Core Features · Product Value

Seven features.
One coherent product.

01
Financial Dashboard
The home screen gives users an immediate, accurate view of their financial position — balance, spending rate, and savings progress — without requiring bank connection. Manual entry with smart categorisation handles the majority of the target segment's behaviour.
PM rationale: First-session value. No onboarding friction.
02
Budget Tracker
Category-based monthly budgeting with progress bars and colour-coded status. Designed for irregular income: users can set flexible or income-proportional budgets rather than fixed monthly caps. The most-requested feature in user research.
PM rationale: Core retention driver. Daily active use hook.
03
Transaction Tracking
Manual transaction entry with smart category suggestions based on merchant type and past behaviour. Weekly and monthly summaries surface spending patterns the user was previously unaware of. The insight layer is where the behaviour change happens.
PM rationale: Solves the core problem. Data engine for future AI.
04
Savings Goals
Named, visual savings goals (emergency fund, holiday, new phone) with progress tracking and micro-contribution suggestions. Goal-framing transforms saving from 'what's left over' into an active, motivated behaviour — the most effective behavioural design intervention available.
PM rationale: Emotional engagement. Retention anchor. Referral trigger.
05
Financial Education
Contextual, bite-sized financial tips triggered by user behaviour — not a generic learning library. If a user's grocery budget hits 80%, they see a tip about meal planning. If they've created 3 savings goals, they unlock a module on emergency funds. Education as a product feature, not a tab.
PM rationale: Differentiation. Loyalty driver. Low technical cost.
06
Smart Alerts
Proactive push notifications at meaningful financial moments: budget category at 70%, unusual spending spike, savings goal on track or behind, weekly review prompt. The system intervenes before the crisis, not after. Designed to be useful, not annoying — with user-controlled frequency.
PM rationale: Behaviour change mechanism. Re-engagement loop.
07
Optional Bank Sync
Bank connection is V2 — deliberately. V1 builds trust through manual entry and immediate value delivery. When bank sync is introduced, it appears as an optional upgrade that removes friction, not a required step that adds it. Connection rate target: 40%+ after 30 days of manual use.
PM rationale: Trust first. Data quality follows adoption.

Success Metrics · Proposed KPIs

What success looks like.

The following KPIs are proposed targets based on comparable consumer fintech products at similar stages. They are not current performance data — Monevol 360 is a product concept. These are the metrics I would set as PM for V1 launch.

30-Day Retention
≥45%
Target for D30 retention. Consumer fintech benchmark is 35–40%. Monevol's daily-use design should outperform.
WAU / MAU Ratio
≥60%
Weekly active users as a share of monthly actives. Indicates genuine daily financial habit, not occasional check-in.
Budget Adherence Rate
≥55%
Share of active users ending the month within their budget in ≥3 categories. Core outcome metric — actual behaviour change.
Savings Goal Completion
≥25%
Share of users completing at least one savings goal within 90 days. Strongest predictor of long-term retention and NPS.
Bank Connection Rate (V2)
≥40%
Target adoption of optional bank sync after 30 days of manual use. Lower friction when trust is established first.
Education Engagement
≥3 tips/wk
Average contextual tip interactions per active user per week. Signals financial literacy progression and platform trust.
Growth Loops & Monetisation

How Monevol grows.
How Monevol earns.

Growth mechanisms
Designed for organic growth within social and economic networks — not paid acquisition.
Goal sharing: Users celebrate milestone achievements — natural referral trigger within trusted networks
Streaks & habits: Daily check-in streaks create return behaviour and app identity
Weekly insights: Automatically generated spending summaries users want to share
Community loops: Shared savings challenges between friends and family groups
Monetisation pathways
Freemium model with trust-based upgrades. Monetisation follows adoption — never blocks it.
Freemium core: Free forever for basic budgeting, transactions, and 2 savings goals
Premium subscription: Unlimited goals, advanced analytics, personalised financial coaching (~$4–6/mo)
Financial marketplace: Contextual referrals to insurance, microloans, and savings products (affiliate revenue)
Data insights (B2B): Aggregated, anonymised spending trend reports for financial institutions

Product Learnings

What this project taught me.

Complexity is a product decision, not a feature list
Every feature we didn't include in V1 was a deliberate choice. The most valuable prioritisation exercise was identifying what to defer — not what to build. Building less, but building it well, is the correct strategy for a behaviour-change product targeting low financial literacy users.
Research doesn't just validate — it reframes the problem
We entered research thinking the core problem was 'users don't save'. We left knowing the core problem was 'users don't see where their money goes'. That reframing changed every product decision — from IA structure to the home screen hierarchy to which feature came first.
Trust is a product feature in financial services
Users in the target segment had high financial anxiety and low institutional trust. Every design decision — from optional bank connection to transparent data language — had to be evaluated for its trust impact before its functional impact. In fintech for underserved users, trust architecture matters more than feature architecture.
98% task success doesn't mean the product is done
Usability testing results were strong — 98% task completion across the prototype. But the deeper finding was that users succeeded at the tasks while still expressing uncertainty about how the product would fit into their daily life. Usability is a baseline, not a differentiator. Habit integration is the real test.
Where This Applies

Why Monevol 360 matters
beyond this project.

The discovery method here — not the fintech vertical — is what transfers. Useful context whether you're scoping a consulting engagement or evaluating a senior PM hire.

Payments & collections fintech
Direct debit, recurring collections, and SEPA-equivalent systems all serve the same problem: making complex payment infrastructure simple for end users. Monevol's user research methodology — interviewing across economic segments, identifying behavioural blockers — is the same discipline.
Consumer & digital banking products
Building for underserved users requires the same PM skills as building for any user — discovery, prioritisation, metric definition, and ruthless scope management. The constraint of zero tolerance for complexity produces better product instincts than building for sophisticated users.
Engagement & retention products
Growth loops, habit formation, contextual nudges, and behavioural engagement design are increasingly central to fintech retention strategy. Monevol was designed around these principles from the first interview — not retrofitted after MVP.
