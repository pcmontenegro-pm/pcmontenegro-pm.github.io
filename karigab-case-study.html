Executive Summary

A system, not a tool
— shipped in a week.

A Bolivian fashion retailer's TikTok Live sales channel was outrunning its own operations — duplicate orders, untraceable payments, no unified inventory between physical store and live channel. The team was non-technical and had zero budget for new software.

Rather than recommend a SaaS tool, I designed a complete operating system inside WhatsApp and Google Sheets — a garment coding scheme, payment reference protocol, and unified inventory logic. Delivered end-to-end in 7 days. Daily operational workload dropped 85%, at zero tooling cost.

At a glance
  • Constraint: non-technical team, zero budget, live operations couldn't stop
  • Trade-off: resisted the obvious SaaS fix in favour of a system built on tools they already had
  • Stakeholders: store owner (operations & risk), live-show host (speed), customers (trust at checkout)

Context

A physical store
going digital.

Karigab is a fashion retailer based in Bolivia with an established physical store. Recognising the reach of TikTok Live, the team began hosting daily live shows where a host presents garments directly to thousands of viewers — a format that turned followers into buyers overnight.

The channel worked. Orders came in. But the operational backbone hadn't caught up. Every sale was managed manually across WhatsApp, mental notes, and spreadsheets that didn't talk to each other.

The challenges
  • Duplicate orders — two customers claiming the same item with no stock lock
  • No unified inventory — physical store and TikTok channel operating in silos
  • Payment validation done by memory — no systematic check against bank movements
  • 30+ WhatsApp messages per live session, each answered manually from scratch
  • No operational visibility — the owner had no daily view of revenue, pending orders or stock risk
  • Non-technical team — any solution had to work with WhatsApp and Google Sheets only
Approach

Systems thinking over
tool recommendations.

The instinct in these projects is to reach for a SaaS tool. I didn't. The constraint — a non-technical team, zero budget, live operations that couldn't stop — meant the solution had to be built around what already existed: WhatsApp, Google Sheets, and a functioning physical store.

The work started with the operational flow, not the technology. Understanding exactly where time was lost, where errors were introduced, and where the two channels (physical and digital) diverged.

"The real problem wasn't the tools. It was the absence of a system. The tools were already there."
Observation from discovery — Week 1
01
SKU system & physical labelling
Designed a four-part garment code (Category · Colour · Size · Number) printable on standard A4. Every item gets a unique identifier before it enters inventory. The code travels from the physical tag to the WhatsApp conversation to the spreadsheet row.
Inventory · Operations
02
WhatsApp-native checkout flow
Redesigned the full customer journey within WhatsApp Business — six response templates replacing all manual writing, a pre-assigned order number used as payment reference, and a structured conversation protocol the team could follow without training.
UX · Payment Flow
03
Unified inventory — one source of truth
Built a Google Sheets architecture with two linked tabs: Orders and Stock. Columns for WA Sales and Physical Store Sales feed a single Available figure. The physical team syncs once at close of day — enough to keep the live channel accurate.
Data · Inventory
04
Bank reconciliation by batch
Designed a semi-automated match process: the operator enters the bank-received amount in a single column — the MATCH formula compares it against the order total and returns MATCH ✅, REVIEW ⚠️, or PENDING. Two 15-minute rounds per day replace 30+ individual checks.
Payment Validation · Reconciliation
05
Operations dashboard
A Summary tab showing the owner — in under 2 minutes each morning — today's revenue, unconfirmed orders, match exceptions, and critical stock alerts. Pending items disappear automatically when resolved. No manual curation needed.
Visibility · Reporting
06
Role protocols & team playbook
Defined three operational roles — owner, TikTok host, and WhatsApp operator — each with a daily checklist, clear scope, and permissions in the shared spreadsheet. The system works because ownership is explicit, not assumed.

Why this matters

The broader relevance.

This project sits at the intersection of three dynamics shaping payments globally.

Social commerce is the new checkout
TikTok Live, Instagram Shops, and WhatsApp Commerce are where a growing share of transactions in emerging markets begin — and often complete. Building payment rails for this channel is the next frontier for fintechs expanding beyond traditional e-commerce.
Bank reconciliation without APIs
In markets where banks don't offer open APIs, merchants still need a way to verify that money arrived. This project demonstrates a structured, scalable reconciliation approach that works today — and points to where automation can be layered in as infrastructure matures.
Omnichannel for businesses without platforms
The unified inventory problem — one source of truth for physical and digital sales — isn't unique to Karigab. It's the same challenge facing thousands of growing retailers across Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia who operate outside formal e-commerce platforms.
Product learnings

What this project
reinforced.

Constraints produce better design
No budget, no technical team, no downtime allowed. Each constraint eliminated an option and forced a simpler, more resilient solution. The result is a system anyone on the team can operate and maintain independently.
The reference field is the whole trick
Asking customers to include the order number (KAR-047) in their bank transfer reference reduced payment matching time from 5 minutes per transaction to under 20 seconds. One field. Massive operational leverage.
Batch beats real-time at low volume
With 30+ daily payments across multiple banks, real-time validation per transaction was operationally impossible. Two 15-minute batch rounds proved more reliable and less error-prone than reactive individual checks.
Roles before tools
The system only works because ownership is explicit. Defining who does what — host, operator, owner — before assigning any tool access meant the spreadsheet permissions and protocols matched actual workflow. No gaps, no overlaps.
