The full arc

A constraint became
a product thesis.

Karigab is a Bolivian fashion retailer running daily TikTok Live sales shows. Their team was non-technical, their budget was zero, and their operations were breaking under their own volume — duplicate orders, no unified inventory, payment validation done from memory.

I didn't recommend a SaaS tool. I designed a complete operating system inside WhatsApp and Google Sheets — delivered in 7 days, operational workload down 85%, zero tooling cost. But the more interesting result was what the system proved: that the operational logic for social commerce retail is specific, teachable, and repeatable — and that no existing tool had productised it.

Órbita OS is what came next: the same operational logic — order lifecycle, inventory movements, payment reconciliation, dispatch — built as a multi-tenant SaaS platform. Karigab is its first pilot tenant. The manual system's operator is now running the same logic inside the product.

At a glance
  • Phase 1 — Karigab (2025): constraint-first operations system in WhatsApp + Google Sheets. 7 days, −85% workload, Bs.0 cost.
  • Phase 2 — Órbita OS (2026): multi-tenant SaaS platform generalising the same logic for any social commerce business.
  • Key decision: rejected SaaS tools twice — first because the client couldn't use them, then because none had solved the right problem.
  • What connects them: the same design constraint — mobile-first, radically simple, operable by a non-technical team.
Phase 1 · 2025
Karigab — WhatsApp + Google Sheets
The problem

A physical store
going digital — fast.

Karigab had an established physical store in Bolivia. When they started hosting daily TikTok Live shows, their sales channel scaled overnight. Their operations didn't. Every order was managed manually across WhatsApp, mental notes, and spreadsheets that didn't talk to each other.

The result: duplicate orders from two customers claiming the same item with no stock lock. No unified inventory between the physical store and the TikTok channel. Payment validation done from memory. 30+ WhatsApp messages per live session answered manually from scratch. And no daily visibility for the owner — no revenue figure, no order status, no stock risk.

"The real problem wasn't the tools. It was the absence of a system. The tools were already there."
Observation from discovery — Day 1
What was built

Six components.
One system.

The constraint — non-technical team, zero budget, live operations that couldn't stop — meant the solution had to be built around what already existed. Every decision was tested against one question: can a non-technical operator run this alone?

01
SKU system & physical labelling
A four-part garment code (Category · Colour · Size · Number), printable on standard A4. Every item gets a unique identifier before it enters inventory. The code travels from the physical tag to the WhatsApp conversation to the spreadsheet row.
Inventory · Operations
02
WhatsApp-native checkout flow
Six response templates replacing all manual writing. A pre-assigned order number used as payment reference. A structured conversation protocol the team could follow without training.
UX · Payment Flow
03
Unified inventory — one source of truth
Two linked Google Sheets tabs (Orders/Stock) feeding one Available figure across physical and digital channels. The physical team syncs once at close of day — enough to keep the live channel accurate.
Data · Inventory
04
Batch bank reconciliation
A MATCH formula returning MATCH ✅, REVIEW ⚠️, or PENDING. Two 15-minute rounds per day replace 30+ individual per-payment bank checks. The operator enters one amount — the system flags the rest.
Payment Validation · Reconciliation
05
Operations dashboard
A Summary tab showing the owner — in under 2 minutes each morning — today's revenue, unconfirmed orders, match exceptions, and critical stock alerts. Pending items clear automatically when resolved.
Visibility · Reporting
06
Role protocols & team playbook
Three explicit operational roles — owner, TikTok host, WhatsApp operator — each with a daily checklist, clear scope, and permissions in the shared spreadsheet. Ownership explicit, not assumed.
Operations · Team Design
How it was built

Seven days.
Zero downtime.

The store couldn't pause operations. The system was designed and handed over in parallel with daily live sessions — each piece validated in real conditions before the next was built.

Discovery
Day 1–2
Mapping the real operational flow
Documented the complete as-is process: TikTok live → WhatsApp → payment → dispatch. Timed each step. The primary finding: stock verification and payment matching were consuming over 60% of daily operational time.
Design
Day 3–4
System architecture & flow redesign
Designed the SKU structure, the Google Sheets schema (Orders + Stock + Summary tabs), the WhatsApp conversation protocol, and the bank reconciliation logic. Every decision tested against one constraint: can a non-technical operator run this alone?
Build
Day 5–6
Spreadsheet build & template setup
Built the Google Sheets system with formulas, conditional formatting, data validation, and the MATCH reconciliation logic. Configured WhatsApp Business with six response templates. Labelled the first batch of 30 garments for the next live session.
Launch
Day 7
First live with the system running
The team ran their first TikTok Live with the new system. The post-live workload — previously 6+ hours — was completed in under 2.5 hours including bank reconciliation.
Phase 2 · 2026
Órbita OS — Multi-tenant SaaS
The thesis

The manual system proved
a product existed.

Once the Karigab system was running, the pattern was obvious: every small business selling through TikTok Live, Instagram, or WhatsApp faces exactly the same operational breakdown at exactly the same scale. The problem is specific enough to be solvable. No existing tool — ERP, CRM, e-commerce platform — had solved it for this format.

Órbita OS is the productised version. Not accounting software, not an ERP, not a CRM. Scope: everything from 'customer shows interest' to 'order delivered' — the exact operational surface the manual Karigab system covered, now as a reusable product any social commerce business can run.

"Karigab didn't just need a system. It proved one was worth building."
Decision point — after phase 1 delivery
What Órbita OS does

The same operational logic,
built for any business.

01
Sales — the operational core
Order lifecycle from draft through delivered, with a strict state machine and full status timeline. Only valid next steps show — no dead-end states. The same logic as the WhatsApp checkout flow, now inside a purpose-built interface.
Orders · State Machine
02
Inventory — movement-based
Stock is never edited directly. It's derived from logged movements — ingreso, reserva, venta, ajuste, devolución. The same principle as Karigab's MATCH-formula reconciliation, now systematised. Tapping a product shows its full movement history.
Inventory · Audit Trail
03
Dashboard — 2-minute visibility
Today's revenue, active orders, pending balance, low stock, active shipments — each as a tappable card linking to its section. The same morning-visibility goal as Karigab's Summary tab, now live and automatic.
Visibility · Reporting
04
Multi-tenant by design
Every business uses the same application, never a separate deployment. Data isolation enforced entirely by RLS policy — company_id at the row level. One platform, many businesses, zero data leakage between tenants.
Architecture · Security
05
Payments & Dispatch
Payment records linked to orders. Dispatch and shipping status tracked as part of the same order lifecycle — no separate tool needed. The full post-sale flow in one place.
Payments · Fulfilment
06
Internal admin — tenant management
A separate internal platform for managing tenant companies, plans, and support access — including scoped impersonation for support, logged to an audit trail. Operations at the platform level, separate from the product used by clients.
Platform Ops · Admin

Why this matters

The broader relevance.

This work sits at the intersection of three dynamics shaping commerce and product development right now.

Social commerce is the new checkout
TikTok Live, Instagram, and WhatsApp Commerce are where a growing share of transactions in emerging markets begin — and often complete. The operational infrastructure for this channel doesn't yet exist as a standard product. That's the gap Órbita fills.
Constraint-first beats feature-first
The Karigab system proved that understanding a team's real operational constraint produces better outcomes than selecting software from a feature list. That principle shaped both the manual system and the product that came from it.
Manual before automated
Running the operational logic manually for a real client before building it as software was the most efficient possible validation. It meant Órbita's core modules — sales lifecycle, inventory movements, payment reconciliation — were derived from a system that worked, not specced from assumptions.
